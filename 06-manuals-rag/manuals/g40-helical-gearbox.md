# G-40 Helical Gearbox: Operation and Maintenance Manual

Fictional equipment, written for training. Model G-40, two-stage parallel shaft helical gearbox for conveyor drives.

## 1 Safety

Isolate and lock out the drive motor and secure the driven machine against back-driving before any work. Conveyors can run backwards under load when the brake is released.

## 2 Specifications

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Ratio | 12.5 : 1 |

## 3 Installation

Mount the gearbox on a flat, rigid base; flatness within 0.1 mm. Tighten the foot mounting bolts (M20, grade 8.8) to 390 Nm. Tighten the output coupling bolts to 85 Nm.

Fit the breather plug supplied, in the position marked on the housing, before filling with oil. The gearbox is shipped with a solid transport plug that must be replaced.

## 4 Lubrication

Use extreme pressure (EP) gear oil of viscosity grade ISO VG 220. Fill to the centre of the oil level sight glass.

The first oil change is due after 500 operating hours, to remove running-in wear particles. After that, change the oil every 5,000 operating hours or every 12 months, whichever comes first. The magnetic drain plug should be cleaned at every oil change; record the amount of debris on it.

## 5 Operating limits

Maximum oil sump temperature in continuous operation is 90 °C. Above 100 °C the oil degrades quickly; stop the drive and investigate. Bearing temperature, measured on the housing, should not exceed 80 °C.

## 6 Inspection

Monthly: check for oil leaks at the shaft seals, check the oil level, and listen for changes in noise. Every 6 months: check the breather is clear, check the foot bolts are tight, and take an oil sample for analysis.

## 7 Troubleshooting

### 7.1 Unusual noise

A regular knocking at the input shaft speed usually indicates a damaged bearing. A high-pitched whine that rises with load usually indicates gear tooth wear or incorrect backlash. Grinding noise points to contaminated oil or a failing bearing. Check the oil level and condition, then take a vibration measurement and compare with the baseline taken at commissioning.

### 7.2 Overheating

Common causes: oil level too high (churning losses) or too low, wrong oil grade, blocked breather causing pressure build-up and seal leaks, overload, or insufficient air flow around the housing. Keep the housing clean; a layer of dust insulates the gearbox.

### 7.3 Oil leaks at shaft seals

Usually caused by a blocked breather, worn seal lips or a scored shaft. Clear the breather first. Replace seals with the shaft surface polished; if the shaft is grooved, fit a wear sleeve.
